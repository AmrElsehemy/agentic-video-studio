## What the analytics should be asked later

Whether `satellite`, `satellite-swipe`, `chart-cartogram`, `day-night`, `flow`, `elevation-profile` and a counting `ring` move views and retention against the old-style episodes, once enough of batch 2 to 5 are public (the comparison starts around 26 Oct for batch 2 and 3).

## Rules that stay the same

- Every number on screen comes from a pinned research claim, or is computed from pinned data and said to be. Satellite pictures show only what they show; no cause is stated that they cannot back.
- A counting ring says "countries and territories"; a flow arc's width is only drawn from stated numbers; a profile says its height is stretched.
- Disputed borders get a borders review from you (episode 5 shows the Strait of Hormuz, and Iran and the UAE dispute three islands in it: it stays framed on the strait, not on the islands).
- Nothing is voiced or rendered with sound until you approve the storylines and the silent previews.

## Open checks before drafting

- Each Wikipedia revision has to be fetched and read; figures I am remembering (Point Nemo's 2,688 km, the Cristo Redentor pass at 3,832 m, Badwater and Whitney heights, Tromsø's polar night dates, the Hormuz share of oil) are dropped unless the pinned page says them.
- Episode 5's numbers come from the US Energy Information Administration; if the page cannot be pinned the arcs go in plain, with no figures.
- Episode 7 and 8: night-lights imagery shows light, not people or wealth; the scripts say "light at night" and nothing about the causes beyond what the sources state.
- Episode 10: Lake Poopó's extent varies with the seasons and rains, so the swipe uses two dates a season apart on purpose and says so.
