# PokePulses style guide

The writer, visual director and critics read this before every episode. Rules in the prompts and checks still apply; this is the taste on top of them.

## What the show is
One Pokémon per episode, one surprising idea about it, told in 24 to 45 seconds to a fan scrolling Shorts. The viewer should finish thinking "I didn't know that" and want to argue about the last question.

## Story
- One idea per episode, stated in the hook and paid off at the end. Cut any fact that doesn't push it.
- Lead with tension, not trivia: a contradiction, a cost, a rule the Pokémon breaks.
- Compare to things fans know: its evolutions, its type matchups, the starter it's usually picked against.
- Never overclaim. If the research says "can", don't say "always". If a fact is community lore rather than official, say so or leave it out.
- End on a real either/or the audience can split on, not a quiz with a right answer.

## Voice and tone
- A knowledgeable fan telling a friend something surprising. Quick, warm, a little playful.
- Short, spoken sentences. Contractions. Punch the surprising word.
- Never a documentary narrator, a hype announcer or a lecture. No "Did you know…?" openers, no "Let's dive in", no "Fun fact:".
- Numbers are said the way people say them: "about 7 kilos", not "6.9 kilograms".

## Visuals
- The subject's official artwork is the hero of every scene. Only the episode's own Pokémon is shown; others are named, never drawn.
- Each scene shows one idea. A primitive (counter, meter, bars, type shift, timeline, checklist) is for a scene whose idea can be seen; otherwise the default artwork shot is right.
- One to three primitives per episode. The last scene always keeps its verdict layout.
- Colour comes from the Pokémon: palette accents follow its official colours on a dark background, with high-contrast ink.
- Headlines are short, uppercase-friendly and don't repeat the narration word for word.

## Motion
- Reveals land on the beat; motion settles with a small overshoot, never a bounce.
- A hidden subject is revealed once, with one clean cut. No flashing, no strobing, no shake.

## Never
- Show another Pokémon's artwork, or fan art.
- Invent a move, stat, date or lore detail. Every number traces to the research.
- Use the Pokédex number unless the story is about the number.
- Imitate a known voice or character.
